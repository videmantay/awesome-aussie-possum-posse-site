CHAPTER 1
Have you ever heard tales of the Wild West? You might be familiar with names like Wyatt Earp, Buffalo Bill, and even the notorious Billy The Kid. Doc Holliday might ring a bell, too. But let me tell you about a group you probably haven’t heard of—the Awesome Aussie Possum Posse -try saying that eight times fast - and that’s a crying shame because they were the West’s greatest heroes. 
Right about now, you’re probably thinking , “If they were so great why haven’t I ever heard of them?”  Well, for starters, they came from far away. Yes, Australia! It seems history doesn't often celebrate the outsiders who help shape a nation’s destiny. Or maybe it's because they weren't people at all. They were possums! And it's rare for animals to get credit for their heroic deeds. But ask any squirrel, rabbit, or opossum in the know, and they'll share tales of the Awesome Aussie Possum Posse. Without these valiant possums, America might not be the same—or even exist! Our country could have been a whole lot smaller. Curious yet? You're probably wondering, "What were a bunch of Australian possums doing in the U.S.?" Sit tight, because that’s where our story begins. 

The year was 1873, and over in the city of brotherly love—Philadelphia—there was a buzz of excitement. The city was about to open its very first zoo! Sure, the zookeeper could have settled for local critters, but he dreamed bigger. He wanted exotic animals from every corner of the Earth and was willing to pay handsomely for them. That call went out far and wide, luring animal catchers from all over with their ships brimming with extraordinary animals. Our adventure kicked off on one such ship making the long journey from Australia. The creatures on board were crammed into cramped crates, enduring a treacherous and dreadfully uncomfortable voyage. Sadly, some animals didn’t survive the journey. Others fell ill—poor things! As for the rest, they faced hunger and neglect. You know sometimes people can be plain greedy when it comes to getting money, and animal poachers are some of the greediest people around. It seems greed can make some humans lose sight of kindness, and those hunters were focused solely on wealth.    
Most of the animals didn’t think they would make it out alive, but then one day -  sweet salvation! But sometimes the luckiest of days start out as the yuckiest of days.
You see,  the ship was caught in one of the worst storms ever to hit the Pacific Ocean, and before the captain could make it safely to shore, the ship hit some rocks and began to take on water. The ship began to sink, and as the crew scrambled to save the larger animals like kangaroos and koalas, the “lesser” ones were forgotten. One such animal was Brennen, a bushy-tailed young possum with a spirit of determination. As the water rose like a summer heatwave, he wrestled with his cage door. It swung open just as the deluge hit, tumbling him about as if he were caught in rapids. When he came to, he found himself on a sandy beach, crates scattered around, all marked for the Philadelphia Zoo. "Caged again, this time forever," he thought with a heavy heart.
Brennen was troubled by all the commotion around him. He saw  screaming animals looking for safety. Them critters were running back and forth, this way and that , doing their best to dodge the humans who had kept them in their cages for so long.
“Hello?” a distant voice called out.
Brennen snapped out of his stupor and bolted in the direction of the voice. “Where are you?” 
	A small hand jutted out from the sand like a leaping lizard. Brennen yanked it as hard as he could. 
“Ouch! I'm still in my box!” the voice complained. 
“Who are you?” Brennen asked as he cleared away the sand. 
“I don't have a name,” said the voice. 
Brennen was finally able to see the crate’s latch after clearing away enough sand. He worked the lock as quickly as he could and opened the door. “You're free!” Brennen shouted in triumph when all of a sudden, a wave came crashing over him.  Once again Brennen found himself being tossed to and fro underwater.  When the wave had ebbed, Brennen found himself under the box lying face down. 
That was when a  small, portly, young possum calmly mozied out of the box. He had a label in hand which he read, "Oh, apparently I'm Pygmy, Pygmy Possum," and then he tossed the label away. 
 “A little help, mate,” Brennen said, holding out his hand. Pygmy looked down at him and then as quick as a whip, he wrapped his tail, which was the size of a city square, around Brennen's arm and catapulted him clear across the beach with one quick flick.
Brennen landed headfirst in the sand with a thud. He shook his head to stop the church bells from ringing and the earth from spinning backwards. Once he got his wits about him, Brennen quickly realized that there were still more animals that needed his help.  “Hey, everybody, this way,” he called to them. 
A nearby crowd of critters began racing towards him like a stammering stampede of stallions.
“No, everyone, come to the tree hollow over here, and don't leave your children behind,” commanded another voice. 
The crowd of critters stopped dead in its tracks and began running in the opposite direction towards the tree hollow that the mysterious voice had indicated. 
“No way, you’ll be sitting ducks in there. There’s only one way in or out. Come towards this thornbush. They won’t get you here,” Brennen shouted at them.
Once again, the crowd stopped and hurried back towards the thorn bush Brennen had told them to go to.
“What?! That is about as outlandish an idea as I could think of.  No way!  Get to the tree hollow,” the voice shot back.
 	 A flabbergasted Brennen shouted back, “Listen, that is going into the lion’s den. This thornbush is your only chance.” 
“Only chance?” the voice shouted back, “Only chance to prick yourself to death!”
Brennen laughed, “And that tree hollow? You might as well be putting them into cages yourself... No, this thornbush….”
The confused crowd of critters’ heads spun like twirling tops, trying to decide who they should listen to. 
The debate was cut short by the arrival of the crew with nets at the ready. "Run!" both Brennen and the mysterious voice yelled. But it was  too late because most of the animals were being snatched up and locked away.
Brennen ran to rescue whoever he could. He bit the ankle of a poacher who had just captured a rather dazzling damsel of a possum. The possum poacher spun around to see who had bitten him, but in that fraction of a second, Brennen had already scampered up his leg to his back, and then he was on his arm where the little lady was clasped in a tight grip.
“Don't worry little lady, I’ve come to your rescue,” exclaimed Brennen, striking a heroic pose. Right then, he was snatched up by the poacher’s free hand.
“Oh,  you’re rescuing me ?” the pretty possum puckered. “I suppose you'll just fly us right out of here then?” she scoffed.  “Oh, that’s right, you can’t.”  With that, the damsel bit her captor’s hand, and with a loud shriek, he let her drop.
	The damsel spread her arms as she fell,  revealing her sleek gliding membranes.
	“Crickey, you're a glider!” exclaimed Brennen.
	“Not just any glider: Gretel the Greatest Greater Glider,” she boasted, and with a sudden gust of wind, she was out of harm’s way. 
	Our would-be hero found himself in a bad situation as he was caught in the flattening fist of a ferocious foe. Brennen was met with a gruesome gaze from the grumpy, grunting goon. 
Brennen was about to meet his end when another possum appeared from nowhere and plopped perfectly on the grumpy goon´s groin. “Crickey!” Brennen shouted as the goon fell to the floor, taking his captive with him. 
Now on the ground, the goon’s grip loosened, and Brennen, quite debonair, wiggled his way out and dusted himself off, looking around for someone to thank.    
“You were nearly a goner there,” a googly-eyed ringtail possum said in between giggles.  
“Yeah, thanks mate. What’s the name?” Brennen asked the possum.
“I’d never actually met the bloke. I just saw that he was giving you big trouble,” the ring-tailed said. 
“Well… it wasn’t as bad as it seemed but thank you all the same,” said Brennen. “I’m Brennen.”
“Hi there Brennen. Man, you were in a pickle. Lucky, I saw you when I did,” laughed the possum whose chestnut fur was still soaked, most likely because he had just come off the ship. 
“Yeah, yeah, much appreciated. Though I’m sure, I would have found a way out… um… your name?” 
“Who me? I’m Remy Rodrick,” said the ringtail.
Just as the two shook hands, a wild, white possum came charging in, hotter than a jalapeno in hot sauce. “Which one of you is the boorish, brash-brained bogan who was shouting counter orders? You have just sent everyone to the slaughter,” she bellowed. “I had everything under control and would’ve gotten them to safety!”
A happy, wholesome, honey possum who was bringing up the rear nodded her head, “I’d have to agree.”
“So, you were the one telling everyone to go toward the tree hollow. You imbecile!” Brennen shouted.
 	“I have a mind to send you to the moon right about now.” The white possum put up her fist, ready for a Texas tango .  
Remy grabbed the fist and began shaking, “Hi, I’m Remy Rodrick, and you are?”
“Willow. Willow Willamena,” said the white possum as she snatched her hand away. “This is Hanna Holly,” she said, pointing to the small honey possum.
“Nice to make your acquaintance,” squeaked Hanna, “And who are you?” She asked Brennen.
“Sorry to interrupt, but would it be possible to resume this conversation once we are a safe distance from the approaching poachers?” said Pygmy Possum as he waddled up to the group.
“Everyone, head to the tree hollow,” shouted Willow.
“Are you mad? Everyone let’s get to the thorn bush,” objected Brennen.
“Not this again,” shouted Hanna. 
“Pipe down Hanna, I’ll get us out of here.” Then, turning back to Brennen, Willow continued, “but I suppose you have other ideas.” 
“The humans want to take us to the Philadelphia Zoo,” Brennen explained. “If they catch us, we are going to be locked up for life.” 
“Yeah. So, what’s the plan?” Willow insisted again.
Brennen looked around and saw a small band of approaching poachers, “Just one.  Run!” he said, pointing in the opposite direction.
	 The possums took off faster than a hen in a fox den, heading towards an endless land of sand and heat.    All of a sudden, Gretel Glider gracefully grounded in front of them.  “Where are you geniuses heading? There’s nothing that way but sand for kilometers.  If you have any chance of survival, you need to head to a town I saw in that direction.”
Willow gave Brennen a stare that could even turn Medusa to stone, “Come on then, lead the way,” she said to Gretel. Gretel took off again after catching a gust of wind. 
***
Now for anyone who’s never been in the southern part of California, well, it gets hotter than a steam engine in Hades. Not too many things last more than a few hours under those scorching conditions, and unfortunately, not even our heroes  could stand that kind of heat. 
The first one to crash was Gretel. Literally. She plopped right from the air like a huge hailstone.  Then Pygmy Possum completely passed out. Brennen and Willow rushed, well actually, they trudged to their aide.  Hanna and Remy followed. 
      	“Water, water,” rasped Gretel.  
      	“We're all thirsty,” said Willow. “but as you can see, there's no water anywhere around here.”
 	“What are you talking about? Look around you,” Hanna exclaimed.
“Oh no, Hanna, not you too,” said Willow. Turning to Brennen, she continued, “The poor thing is seeing mirages.”
“Well, with just a little creativity….” Hanna said before she went to work.  She disappeared into an agave bush and then started to whip back and forth so fast it looked like she was everywhere at once. Within seconds, there were chairs, an awning, and tiny cups filled with thick white liquid, all made from the agave bush.
Eyes bulged and jaws dropped to the floor so wide you’d’ve thought they were train tunnels. To say the other possums were impressed would be the world’s biggest understatement. 
“Where did all this come from?” asked Willow as she took a closer look at the furniture.  
“Right off the bush,” Hanna smiled.
Remy grabbed a chair and shook it, just to make sure it was sturdy and that he wasn’t dreaming. Then he plopped down hard and began bouncing up and down on it and finally ended with a look that it was A-OK to have a seat.   Then he snatched up two cups of the mysterious white liquid and pounded them down. “Why, that’s better than Tasmanian Pepper Berries!” 
 	Hanna had since made herself an apron of leaves and was holding a tray full of leaf cups.  “Plenty more where that came from.” she smiled.
All the others gathered round and sat down to enjoy what they called “Hanna’s Desert Tea.” Just as soon as they began to relax, the earth began to rumble and tumbled like a giant was having a belly ache. 
“Blimey, it’s a stampede,” cried Brennen pointing in the direction of a herd of oncoming bighorn sheep. “And from the looks of it, they’re headed straight at us.”
The oncoming stampede didn’t look to be slowing down either.  When it seemed more likely than not that they would be trampled under a hundred  hooves, both Willow and Brennen shouted out at the same time -- but two very different orders. “Take cover,” bellowed Willow while Brennen shouted at everyone to “Run!”
Willow ducked into a nearby bush while Brennen made like the wind and was halfway to the state line before any of the others even had time to blink. 
When it did finally dawn on the other possums to get to moving, well,  they were like kids under a pinata, tripping all over each other and clawing at the ground, trying to figure out just whose orders to follow.  When all was said and done, they ended up in a tangled pile with Pygmy’s tail roped all around them.
The herd of bighorns came to a sudden stop just a skunk’s hair from trampling the jumbled possum. There was a small silhouette that stood atop of the biggest of the bighorns. 
“Goodness,” exclaimed Hanna from underneath Remy, “It's a striped possum.”
When Willow heard that, she came from behind the bush, “I said to take cover, and you all just stood there like mosquitoes in molasses.”
The striped possum shimmied down the leg of the bighorn and walked past everyone grabbing a leaf-cups of white liquid. He swallowed it all in one gulp and then wiped his mouth. Hunched over to breathe heavily and then grabbed another cup and gulped it down.
“And you are?” asked Willow.
The possum looked around as if just aware that others were staring at him. “Plaid,” he said hoarsely and grabbed the last cup off the tray and began gulping that down too.
“Hey Plaid, you came off that ship the same as us, right?” asked Willow.
“Yep,” he said.
“Are you traveling alone?” Willow asked.
“Yep,” Plaid responded.
“Do you ever answer a question with more than just one word?” snapped Willow.
But Before Plaid could answer, Brennen came huffing back, “You all stood there like a roo in quicksand.  I clearly said to run.” He took a look at Plaid, “And who are you?”
“Plaid” 
“What the devil were you trying to do, kill us all?” Brennen shot back.
“Nope”
“Did you come off the ship the same as us?”
“Yep”
“Don’t you ever answer a question with more than just one word,” shouted Brennen.
“He was riding on top of those sheep,” Remy told Brennen.
“Is that right?” Brennen asked Plaid.
“Yep.”
“We could use the sheep to ride into town.” Pygmy suggested.
“Or,” Willow butted in, “we could use these sheep here to ride into town.”
The others scratched their heads in bewilderment and  wondered if Willow had actually noticed that she just repeated what Pygmy had suggested.  She, on the other hand, dusted herself off and scampered up one of the rams.  Not to be outdone, Brennen got on another ram with slightly bigger horns and a brown blotched muzzle. Then, without a second thought, both of them kicked their big horns and rode away. 
Remy, Gretel, and Plaid scampered up some ewes and rode off after the two would-be leaders. Unfortunately, the two smaller possums didn’t fare as well. 
First of all, climbing the bighorn sheep proved as tough as climbing up an iron post smothered in grease in the pouring rain.  Even when Pygmy had managed to make it up, his ram seemed unaware of his presence.   Pygmy jumped up and down, calling out “Yah! yah!” but the bighorn just continued to graze.
Hanna began scurrying up a leg when, Whamo! She was knocked off because the bighorn scratched what he thought was a bug bite.
The two tiny possums looked at each other and decided they needed a new strategy.  Pygmy noticed some nearby jackrabbits. Using his tail, he lassoed one and wrangled it in, and mounted it.  On the other hand, Hanna had already constructed a stagecoach using a tumbleweed, leaves from an agave bush, and a cactus leaf.  She saw two kangaroo rats passing by and beckoned them with a whistle.  The two approached her as if in a trance, and she hitched them to the coach. “I’ll call you Ranga, and you'll be Koo,” she smiled. Then she got in the coach and flicked the reins but the kangaroo rats didn’t move. 
“Come on now, follow the others as fast as you can,” Hanna ordered. This sent those roo rats running right after the other like a deer from a pack of wolves. The entire gang was now galloping gallantly toward the town.
